# The monetisation of attention

Prior to the launch of ChatGPT in late 2022, the Internet used to actually be usable. You could scroll comment sections on social media to see real replies from real people trying to engage in conversations. Now, sites like Twitter, Instagram and even YouTube are near-unusable.

People posted out of love for their hobbies or to engage in discussion. Now, human attention (or 'impressions' as many sites say) is a valuable asset, one that every bot, automated account or general low-quality individual seeks to take from you. This could be wasting your time with 'ragebait' or recycling the same slop viral posts looking for their monthly paycheck.

# Outsourcing thinking

I've watched many individuals take basic questions and pose it to an LLM model. This prompted me to begin reading academic papers on the topic of the human brain when using LLMs and found an MIT-published paper called 'Your brain on ChatGPT' about how brain activity noticeably lessened when using an LLM for a task, as opposed to either a search engine or no tool at all. 

That being said, finding a paper that reinforces my pre-established beliefs isn't particularly useful; you don't learn anything by being agreed with. Even then, the authors of the paper themselves have said they intentionally didn't use terminology such as 'brain rot' as it's not what the paper's about. It awaits peer-review as they prepare for their next experiment on 'vibe coding'.
